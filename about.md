# About me

I'm Sandy, originally from Australia and now based in Cambridge.

I'm a PhD student in the Department of Genetics and a Harding Distinguished Postgraduate Scholar at Sidney Sussex College. My research combines field surveillance, genomic analysis and mathematical modelling to inform targeted public health responses to *Aedes aegypti*, the mosquito behind dengue, as it emerges across East Africa.

I also like starting things. In Australia, I co-founded the Nice Coffee Company, a not-for-profit that used coffee sales to fund health and education programmes in East Africa. In New Zealand, I helped get Blue Carbon Services off the ground, a project exploring how seaweed can draw down atmospheric carbon and serve as a resilient food source. In Cambridge, I co-founded the Cambridge Biosecurity Hub, a not-for-profit that runs programmes to help experienced researchers and skilled PhDs move into important biosecurity work.

## What I write about

Infectious disease and biosecurity, the realities of research, lessons from building small ventures, and the occasional good book or cup of coffee.
